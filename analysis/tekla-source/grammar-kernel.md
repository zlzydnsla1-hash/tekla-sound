# TEKLA — Grammar Kernel

## GK-01 — Center one product in a plain field

- Input condition: A single hero product (a made bed, a folded product family) needs to read as the brand's default visual statement.
- Transformation: Place the product frontally at the center of the frame against one plain, lightly textured surface, leaving roughly half or more of the frame as surrounding negative space.
- Intended effect: The product reads as calm and considered rather than styled or crowded, before any lifestyle context is added.
- Evidence IDs: EV-102 EV-104
- Confidence: high
- Alternative explanation: Minimal styling could simply reflect e-commerce photography convenience rather than a deliberate key-visual rule.
- Exception and scope: Applies to the current global masterbrand's owned-web hero imagery and flagship storefront window only.
- Protected source expression: Do not copy TEKLA's own photographs; reproduce only the compositional relationship.
- Acceptance test: The primary subject is centered with visibly dominant plain surrounding field.
- Rejection test: Multiple competing subjects or heavy prop styling fill the frame edge to edge.

## GK-02 — Separate label proof from mood photography

- Input condition: A product's material/construction truth and a lifestyle emotional register both need representation.
- Transformation: Give each image exactly one job — either a clinical macro/flat-lay crop showing label, weave, and construction, or a soft, low-key lifestyle portrait — and never merge the two in one frame.
- Intended effect: Construction claims stay verifiable and uncluttered while mood imagery stays emotionally legible, without either undercutting the other.
- Evidence IDs: EV-103 EV-105 EV-106
- Confidence: medium
- Alternative explanation: The separation could be an artifact of using different photographers or shoot days rather than an intentional rule.
- Exception and scope: Applies to captured owned-web product and lifestyle imagery only; campaign video was not assessed.
- Protected source expression: Do not copy TEKLA's specific label typesetting or exact crop; reuse only the separation-of-jobs principle.
- Acceptance test: Every sampled image can be labeled as either a detail/proof shot or a mood shot, not both.
- Rejection test: One image simultaneously shows legible label/construction detail and an active lifestyle scene.

## GK-03 — Repeat the same woven label across every category

- Input condition: A new product category (bedding, bath, slippers) needs a brand-recognition anchor without adding a new graphic system per category.
- Transformation: Apply one rectangular woven label, black all-caps TEKLA wordmark on a light ribbon, with product line and size/spec beneath it, at a consistent seam or vamp location.
- Intended effect: Any product photograph, regardless of category, can be identified as TEKLA from the same small physical detail.
- Evidence IDs: EV-105 EV-106 EV-108
- Confidence: high
- Alternative explanation: Consistent labeling may simply follow generic textile-industry labeling convention rather than a deliberate brand rule.
- Exception and scope: Confirmed for bedding, bath, and slipper categories only; not confirmed for candles or other home-decor items.
- Protected source expression: Do not copy the exact label artwork; reuse only the label-hierarchy and placement principle.
- Acceptance test: The same label hierarchy (wordmark, then line, then spec) appears in a newly sampled product image.
- Rejection test: A sampled product category shows no label or a differently ordered hierarchy.

## GK-04 — Carry one dark identity value across every surface

- Input condition: The same brand needs to be recognizable across a digital screen, a physical storefront, and a sewn product label, none of which share a rendering technology.
- Transformation: Apply the same near-black value (#181818) as text/mark ink on every surface — CSS text and borders on the site, painted or printed signage in the store, and printed wordmark ink on woven labels — against a consistently light ground.
- Intended effect: Identity recognition survives a change of material and medium without requiring a shared font file or asset format.
- Evidence IDs: EV-003 EV-104 EV-105
- Confidence: medium
- Alternative explanation: Near-black ink on a light ground is also just a common, generic print/UI default, not necessarily an intentional brand rule.
- Exception and scope: Confirmed for owned web, the Copenhagen flagship, and sampled woven labels; not confirmed for packaging or other unsampled physical media.
- Protected source expression: Do not copy TEKLA's exact CSS or label artwork; reuse only the cross-medium dark-ink-on-light-ground principle.
- Acceptance test: A newly sampled TEKLA surface uses a near-black mark on a light ground.
- Rejection test: A newly sampled TEKLA surface uses a materially different identity color pairing.
